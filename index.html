About

I am an MPhil student in Computer and Information Engineering at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, advised by Prof. Fangxun Zhong at iHEAR Lab.

My research interests lie in embodied AI, multimodal learning, and 3D vision. I study how agents can ground language in visual and geometric observations, reason about interactions over time, and form structured representations for reliable decision-making.

My current work uses surgical and robotic scenes as challenging testbeds for fine-grained grounding, occlusion, uncertainty, and temporal interaction. Looking ahead, I am interested in general-purpose Physical AI, particularly world models and embodied foundation models for robot perception, prediction, and action.

I am seeking PhD opportunities for Fall 2027.

Selected Research

2026
MARS — Diagnosable Vision-Language Grounding
Research manuscript
MARS develops an explicit intermediate interface between vision-language proposals and precise spatial grounding. By exposing semantic intent, proposal support, image-aligned evidence, and spatial readout as separate states, it makes proposal and localization failures independently testable. The study uses surgical image-space grounding as a precision-sensitive testbed.
2026
Absorb3D — Recovery and Selection in 3D Grounding
Research manuscript
This work studies why recovering additional candidates can improve recall without improving final grounding accuracy. Absorb3D addresses the resulting recovery-selection gap by scoring candidates relative to the alternatives introduced alongside them.
2026
PACE-Eval — Budgeted Procedural Anticipation
Research manuscript
PACE-Eval studies how continuous anticipation scores are converted into discrete alarms under explicit timing, matching, cooldown, and false-alarm constraints. It treats the score-to-alarm policy and its calibration as part of the evaluated anticipation system.
